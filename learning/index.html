Module 2 of 7

Structure tells you which side to be on

Swing highs and swing lows give the trend a shape. Higher highs and higher lows mean buyers are in control. The reverse means sellers are.

Four shapes of the market

HH + HL is an uptrendEach push goes higher and each dip holds above the last dip.
The last swing is the lineThe most recent higher low in an uptrend is where the idea breaks if price closes under it.
Ranges are not trendsPrice bouncing between two levels pays for patience, not for chasing.

Levels are where decisions get made

Price reacts at the same places because many traders watch the same places. Turn each layer on and see how a single morning respects them.

Module 4 of 7

Three setups cover most of the day

Step through each one. Every setup has the same parts: a level, a trigger candle, a stop where the idea is wrong, and a target at least twice the risk.

Step-by-step setups

Breakout and retestWait for the level to flip. Entering on the retest gives a closer stop than chasing the breakout.
Failed breakoutA close back inside the range traps late buyers. Their exits fuel the move the other way.
Trend pullbackBuy the dip to a higher low or VWAP in an uptrend, only after a bullish candle closes.

Module 5 of 7

Turn a chart idea into a contract

The stop lives on the stock chart. The contract only has to be sized so that a stop-out costs what you planned. Move the sliders and watch the contract count change.

Position size calculator

Estimates use delta only. Real fills also move with gamma, theta and implied volatility, so treat the numbers as a planning tool.

Picking the strike
ChoiceTypical deltaCostBehavior on a day trade
In the money0.65 to 0.80HighTracks the stock closely and loses less to theta. Fewer contracts for the same risk.
At the moneyAbout 0.50MediumBalanced. The usual default for a clean level-to-level move.
Out of the money0.15 to 0.35LowNeeds a large, fast move. Cheap contracts win rarely and decay fast, which is how small accounts bleed.
Stop on the stockDecide the exit from the chart, then size the contract to fit it. Never start from the premium.
Size, don't guessContracts = max loss divided by loss per contract at your stop.
Time is a costIf the move needs hours, a 0DTE contract is the wrong tool. Take a later expiry.
