Module 1 of 7

VWAP and volume decide which signals to trust

A setup at a good level can still fail. Two cheap filters remove many of those: which side of VWAP price is on, and whether the breakout candle had real volume behind it.

VWAP: three ways price treats it (blue line)

Same breakout, different volume

Side of VWAP is the biasCalls mostly when price is above and VWAP is rising. Puts mostly when it is below and falling.
Reclaims and rejectionsA close back across VWAP that holds is a signal. A poke that closes back is a warning.
Volume proves the breakCompare the breakout bar to the recent average. About 1.5 times or more is a good working rule.

Module 2 of 7

Zoom out for the where, zoom in for the when

Levels that matter come from the 1-hour chart. Entries come from 5-minute candles reacting at those levels. Flip between the two views.

Top-down view of one level

Mark levels on the 1-hourSwing highs and lows from the last several days carry more weight than a 5-minute wiggle.
Enter on the 5-minuteWait for a closed candle that reacts at the zone. The zone gives the place, the candle gives the timing.
Stops use the small chartThe 5-minute swing gives a tight stop. The 1-hour level gives a realistic target.

Module 3 of 7

How you exit decides the result

Take the same entry and the same price path, and three exit habits give three different results. Pick a path and an exit style, then compare in R, where 1R is the amount you risked.

Same entry, different exits

Plan the exit firstKnow the stop, the first target and the trailing rule before you enter.
Scaling smooths the rideSelling half at 1R banks something and lets the stop move to entry. You give up some of the biggest winners.
No plan is a plan to loseHolding through your stop turns a 1R loss into a bigger one.

Module 4 of 7

What the option does while you wait

A day-trade option is a bet on direction against a clock. Move the sliders on an at-the-money call (strike 600) and watch price, delta, gamma and time decay respond.

At-the-money call, strike 600

Prices come from the Black-Scholes model with a 4 percent rate and no dividends. Real quotes differ because of skew and spreads.

DeltaHow much the option moves per $1 in the stock. Deeper in the money means a higher delta.
GammaHow fast delta changes. It peaks for at-the-money options near expiry, which is why 0DTE moves in jumps.
Theta and IVTime decay speeds up late in the day. A drop in implied volatility, as after news, shrinks the price even when the stock does not move.

Module 5 of 7

Four kinds of day, four different plans

The same setup pays on one type of day and bleeds on another. Learn the clues from the first hour and size your day to match.

Full session, about 10 minutes per candle (blue line is VWAP)
Name the day by 10:30Use the opening range, VWAP crosses and how pullbacks behave.
Chop is the costliestTheta wins on range days. Fewer trades and smaller size beat forcing setups.
Event days are their own typeCPI, jobs and Fed days can invalidate a normal read. Reduce size or wait for the release.
